Per-passage highlighter

Within a document, the 13B score did not single out the passage holding a new finding (top-1 in 11.7% of documents against a bar of 50%); it registers a finding only when a passage is compared with its own no-finding twin, the idea behind the planned automatic-twin claim test.

The question#

In Phase 1b, OLMo-2-13B’s payoff score ranked a passage holding a new finding above a matched passage without it in 95% of pairs. Could the same score show an author which parts of a document are new to the model? Concretely: within one document, does the per-passage score put the paragraph that holds the new finding on top?

What we did#

Terms are defined in the glossary.

  • Documents. The 60 Phase 1b test documents. Each is a real public record of 21–31 passages (about 2,900 tokens). One passage, written for the study, states a finding that is new to the model; three later passages restate or use it. These planted passages stay private.
  • Twins. Each document has a twin in which the finding passage is swapped for a same-story passage with no finding. Nothing else changes.
  • Scores for every passage, from OLMo-2-13B with Phase 1b’s frozen code:
    • payoff (P_corr): how much harder the next 768 tokens are to predict when the passage is removed, with copied words masked;
    • surprise: S_in (bits per token in context), S_cold (alone) and their difference K;
    • two surprise-times-payoff combinations, a quadrant rule and a rank product.
  • Metrics. Where the finding passage ranks among all passages (top-1, top-3, mean reciprocal rank). The same ranks at the same position in the twin, where there is no finding, count false highlights.
  • Reading rule, committed before the run: a score localizes only if its top-1 is at least 0.50, it beats chance and the surprise baselines, and its top-1 in the twins is at most half its top-1 in the real documents. The study is exploratory, not pre-registered.

What we found#

  • Verdict: NO. Payoff ranks the finding first in 11.7% of documents (chance 4.1%) and in the top 3 in 33%.
  • Payoff mostly highlights the document’s opening. In the 53 misses, the top passage is the record’s first passage 55% of the time and comes before the finding 81% of the time. It out-pays the finding by a median 75 bits. Payoff measures how much later text depends on a passage, and setup passages dominate that.
  • The finding registers only as a paired signal. The finding passage out-pays its own twin in 95% of items, exactly as in Phase 1b. But the difference, a median 16 bits (64.2 against 48.3), is small next to the spread of payoffs within one document.
  • Surprise looks better but ignores the finding. S_in puts the finding first in 60% of documents, and the finding-free twin passage first in 57%. Inserted passages are simply more surprising than the record’s own text (6.0 against 3.5 bits per token), with or without a finding.
  • Combinations inherit the surprise artifact. The rank product reaches top-1 0.425, but also 0.317 in the twins. The quadrant classifier flags 1.5 of the record’s own passages per document, so only 26% of its flags hit the finding.

What it means for appraisal#

  • Per-passage “what’s new” maps from this score should not be offered. What the score knows about a finding is a comparison (this passage against the same passage without the finding), not a ranking of passages.
  • Planned fix: an automatic-twin claim test. Keep the comparison that worked and drop the ranking. For each claim in a document, generate its twin automatically: the same passage with that claim removed and everything else unchanged. Then score each passage against its own twin. This is a plan. It has not been built or run.
  • The report adds two untested suggestions. Discount the payoff that setup passages earn by position and length, or replace a passage instead of deleting it. And test surprise on documents whose new findings are natural text, where insertion cannot give them away.

Caveats#

  • Exploratory, with one analyst, no independent verifier, and no correction across the nine scores compared.
  • The planted passages are detectably foreign: more surprising than the record and unhelped by its context. That confounds every surprise-based score here. Documents with natural new findings were not tested, so surprise may still work on real novelty.
  • Ground truth is partial. The records’ own passages may hold content new to the model, and any such top-1 counts as a miss.
  • The setting favors payoff: every finding is followed by a full window and three passages that use it. A real finding near the end of a document, or one never echoed, would earn less.
  • One scorer (13B), one window (768 tokens), one copy rule. With 60 items in 31 clusters, proportions carry intervals of about ±0.1–0.13.

Cost and date#

Run on 2026-10-20. RunPod: $0.52 (cap $5), one A40 GPU for 0.875 pod-hours. Scoring took about 19 s ($0.003) per 2,900-token document, excluding boot and model download.
