Anticipation pilot

One epoch of training on a paper made later papers that cite it slightly more predictable to OLMo-2-13B (p = 9e-5), but the pre-registered three-epoch gate failed and the true paper could not yet be told apart from an altered copy.

The question#

Does training a model on paper A make later papers that cite A more predictable, beyond what any paper in the same field does? If so, later work becomes an outside check on what A taught. And does the true A help more than a copy with its claims altered? That would tie the measure to truth, not just to learnable text.

What we did#

Terms are defined in the glossary.

  • Model. OLMo-2-13B, an open base model whose training data ends before 2025.
  • Papers. 36 papers A in CS/ML and physics, posted 2025-01 to 2026-02, so all are new to the model. Each has one later arXiv paper B (its follow-up) that cites it, 33–399 days later. The items form six blocks of six.
  • Arms. Each run trains a fresh LoRA (rank 16, learning rate 1e-4) on one of:
    • A, scored after epochs 1 and 3;
    • an altered copy of A, made by a frozen script that rescales numbers and swaps comparison words such as “higher” and “lower” (mean 463 edits). The copies were never committed or published;
    • A’s abstract ten times, in filler text;
    • a positive control: the opening of B itself.
  • Measure. Bits per token saved on B’s later text. Words copied from any A in the block are masked (14.5% of tokens). Within a block, every other paper acts as a control. The specific gain T* is A’s gain on its own B, beyond A’s effect on the block’s other papers and beyond what other papers do for B.
  • Pre-registered gate, frozen with the analysis code before any GPU output, all at three epochs:
    • E0: the positive control registers learning;
    • E1: T* is above zero;
    • E2: the true A beats the altered A.

What we found#

  • Gate: FAIL. E0 and E2 pass; E1 fails. Three epochs overfit A. Almost every B became less predictable, and A’s own follow-up suffered most (T* −0.0219 bits per token).
  • At one epoch, the predicted pattern appears. This was a pre-specified descriptive result, not the gate. Every item’s B became more predictable (36 of 36). A’s specific gain was positive: T* +0.0037 bits per token, positive in 28 of 36 items, Wilcoxon p = 9e-5, and stable across seeds. About a third of A’s gain on its follow-up is specific to A; the rest is general field adaptation.
  • True and altered A could not yet be told apart. At one epoch they help B equally (difference +0.0006, interval 0.0000 to +0.0014). At three epochs the true A wins in 75% of items, but both versions leave B worse than no training, and about two thirds of the margin is a generic penalty for altered text. That reads “altered claims hurt more”, not “true claims help”.
  • The panel can see learning. The positive control helped its own B in 35 of 36 items, and its gain was specific to that B.
  • The abstract ×10 arm gave a weak, non-significant specific gain (T* +0.0010, p = 0.12).

What it means for appraisal#

  • “A document teaches knowledge that later work uses” is measurable at 13B, on this exploratory evidence. The effect is small: about +0.004 bits per token on B’s non-copied text.
  • Dose decides the sign. One epoch shows the effect; three epochs reverse it. The damage tracked the number of updates, so the report recommends one epoch or less, a fixed step budget per document, or a decaying learning rate.
  • Truth anchoring is not shown yet. The report’s next steps: score the sentences of B that cite A, add belief probes, and try training on paraphrases.
  • The main experiment later repeated the one-epoch test on 30 fresh papers in OLMo-2-13B and DeepSeek-V3.1. The specific gain replicated in both (T* +0.0042 and +0.0058). Truth on later papers’ text was still not established.

Caveats#

  • Exploratory: 36 items, six clusters (so the bootstrap is coarse), one model, one LoRA recipe, one B per item.
  • “Cites” is not “builds on”. How each B uses A was not judged, and 16 of 36 B share an author surname with their A.
  • The script changes only numbers and comparison words, not methods, theorem conditions or qualitative claims. Four items got very few edits.
  • The scored text mostly comes after B’s introduction, where restatements of A usually sit.
  • E2’s lower end (0.556) sits at its threshold.

Cost and date#

Run on 2026-10-20. RunPod: $2.81 (estimate about $6, cap $10), on four A40 GPUs: 168 LoRA runs plus 8 base scorings, 4.76 pod-hours. No failures, reruns or deviations from the pre-registration.
