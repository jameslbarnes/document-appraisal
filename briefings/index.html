The experiments#

# Briefing Question Answer Compute
1 Phase 1: the cheap score at 1B Can a training-free score spot planted new findings? No at 1B (NO-GO). Larger models looked promising $1.93
2 Phase 1b: 13B and 32B Does it work at larger scale, on fresh items? Yes at 13B; no at 32B, a gap within noise in $61.64
3 H1b: score vs learning Does the score predict what a model learns? No. Correlation 0.28 against a 0.50 bar; a simple baseline beat it in $61.64
4 Navier–Stokes pilot Do an open and a frontier model learn the same things from one landmark paper? Yes after one pass. Not with the same settings at four passes, where the frontier model overfit $14.79
5 Anticipation pilot Does reading paper A help predict the later papers that cite it? Yes. True and falsified versions are not yet separable $2.81
6 Highlighter Can we map which passages are new to a model? Not as built $0.52
7 Main experiment: 30 papers Do these hold across 30 papers and two model families? Open matches frontier and anticipation holds. Truth is not established; there is a unique increment $43.03
8 Navier–Stokes behavior Does one reading change how a model attacks problems? Barely. Knowledge, not method $10.64
9 What a report could look like How should results be presented to a person? Plain words first: what changed, why it matters, how it compares none

How to read a briefing#

Each briefing puts the plain-language finding first and the numbers after it. Intervals are 95% bootstrap ranges. “Pre-registered” means the rule was frozen before scoring; “descriptive” means it was looked at afterwards. Terms are defined in the glossary.
