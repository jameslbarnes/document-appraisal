H1b: does the cheap score predict learning?

No: on 288 segments of real research records, the 13B score predicted the 13B's own LoRA gain only weakly (0.28 against a pre-registered bar of 0.50), and a simpler in-context-gain baseline did better (0.44), so the cheap score cannot stand in for training.

The question#

The 13B score can spot a planted finding (Phase 1b). Does it also predict what the model actually learns from a real document? H1b compared the cheap score with a gold standard: briefly fine-tune the model on each document and measure what it learned.

What we did#

  • Documents. 288 segments of about 2,048 tokens from real public research records (176 streams, four sources, 201 segments from one forum). Each was paired with its held-out continuation: the next ~1,024 tokens of the same record.
  • Cheap score (U). The 13B’s compression progress (how much each passage makes the following text easier to predict, copying excluded), summed over every passage per 1,000 bytes of text.
  • Gold standard and dose. We fine-tuned OLMo-2-13B on each segment with LoRA, which trains a small set of added weights while the model’s own weights stay frozen. Dose (how hard it was trained): 8 steps at learning rate 1e-4, chosen by a pre-registered rule in a 30-unit pilot, with 3 seeds (864 runs). The gain is the drop in surprise on the continuation (bits per token, ignoring words copied from the segment), minus the drop after the same training on matched Wikipedia controls, so the generic effect of training cancels.
  • Pre-registered tests.
    • T4: the rank correlation between U and the gain, computed within each source and pooled (ρ_ws), must reach 0.50 with a lower bound of at least 0.30.
    • T5: U must beat each of 15 cheap baselines significantly (Holm-corrected for multiple tests), and the best of them by at least 0.10.
  • Also reported. U against a 1B learner’s gain (secondary). At James’s request, a RHO-LOSS-style excess loss: a segment’s loss under the learner minus its loss under a larger reference model (exploratory, D45).

What we found#

  • Verdict: NO-GO, “H1b failed: T4/T5.”
  • T4 failed. ρ_ws between U and the gain was 0.278 (95% interval 0.154–0.388), against the bar of 0.50.
  • T5: a baseline did better. The in-context gain (B3: how much better a segment’s passages are predicted in context than alone) reached 0.442, beating U by 0.164. Six more were statistically indistinguishable from U, including word overlap within the segment (0.394). U beat only 5 of the 15 after correction.
  • Not seed noise. The gain was reproducible across seeds (ICC 0.999). Other error sources, such as using one continuation per segment, were not measured.
  • The link is real but modest. Six of eight pre-registered secondary variants were significantly above zero (0.19–0.33); four of eight were if two that rely on post-freeze contamination shares are set aside (D46).
  • Smaller learner. U against the 1B’s gain gave 0.192 (0.066–0.315), below T4’s bar.
  • Excess loss (exploratory). With a 32B reference, it showed no detectable signal for the 13B’s gain (−0.057). With the 1B as learner and the much stronger 13B as reference, it reached 0.271. A pre-specified combination with U did not help (0.144 for the 13B; D50).

What it means for appraisal#

  • You cannot skip training. None of the cheap scores tested reached the 0.50 bar: U reached 0.278 and the best baseline 0.442. As the Phase 1b summary puts it, “detecting planted novelty is not the same as predicting learning.”
  • To know what a document teaches a model, measure it directly with a short fine-tune and matched controls, as the follow-on protocol is designed to do (REPORT_1B.md §10).
  • The yardstick may be part of the story (interpretation). Gain on a segment’s own continuation may reward self-similar records, which the winning baseline may track (untested). A better yardstick would also test transfer, recall of the new content, and truth.

ρ_ws runs from −1 to 1 (0: no relation; 1: same order). ICC is the share of variation in the gain due to the segment rather than the seed. A drop of one bit per token means the model gives each token twice the probability.

Caveats#

  • One dose and one held-out continuation per segment. In the pilot, longer or faster training learned the segment’s facts better but made the continuation worse.
  • Sources were unbalanced. Per-source ρ was 0.352 for the forum (201 segments) and 0.062 for the 72 lab-notebook segments.
  • The two other planned target kinds gave no support: related later documents were not built, and planted-fact probes showed no association detected (−0.042).
  • The Verifier’s replication with new seeds was not run, since no GO was possible (D51).
  • The excess-loss references were off-the-shelf larger models, unlike the held-out-trained reference of RHO-LOSS (Mindermann et al., 2022), and the two configurations also differ in learner.
  • Truth was not tested. By design, the score measures learnable new content, not truth.
