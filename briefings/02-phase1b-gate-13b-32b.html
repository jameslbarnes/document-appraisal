Phase 1b: the gate at 13B and 32B

On 60 fresh planted items, OLMo-2-13B passed a stricter planted gate with a clear margin over word overlap and OLMo-2-32B failed; an exploratory diagnosis reads the 13B–32B difference as item-level noise, not an effect of scale.

The question#

In Phase 1, larger scorers met every gate criterion, but only as diagnostics on items already used. Phase 1b asked, under a new pre-registration: on fresh items, does a 13B or 32B scorer pass a stricter gate that also requires beating word overlap? A pass would license the costly learning test, H1b.

What we did#

  • Models and dose. OLMo-2-13B and OLMo-2-32B, open models named before any new item was scored. Phase 1’s frozen score and copy mask; no training. Outputs stayed sealed (hashed, unopened) until both scorers had finished.
  • Documents and arms. 84 new planted findings on Phase 1’s 84 public base records. Every fact and text was new, and each fact was screened so that six models could not guess it. Arms as in Phase 1: noise, and a same-story control (the finding swapped for a detail nothing later uses). After pre-registered exclusions, 60 test items in 31 clusters remained.
  • Stricter gate (G1b). As before: T1, finding vs noise, by AUROC (the chance that a random finding outscores a random noise passage); T2, finding vs same-story control, by win rate W (the share of items where the finding outscores its control; ties count half); T3, the copy mask must at least halve false alarms on made-up names. New: T2w, the score’s W minus word overlap’s W on the same pairs must have a lower bound above 0. Two scorers meant two chances to pass, so every interval is a 97.5% bootstrap interval instead of 95%.
  • Secondary (M1b). Beat each of the four “is later text about the same thing?” baselines by at least 0.05.

What we found#

  • Verdict: 13B PASS; 32B FAIL (on T2w and T3).
  • 13B. It ranked the finding above its same-story control in 57 of 60 items (W 0.950). Word overlap scored W 0.758 on the same pairs, so the margin was +0.192 (lower bound 0.074). It beat all four relatedness baselines (M1b PASS).
  • 32B. It passed T2 exactly at the bar (W 0.850). Its margin over word overlap (+0.092) had a lower bound below zero, and its copy mask left a false-alarm share of 0.315 against a ceiling of 0.278. M1b was inconclusive.
  • Replicated. A clean-checkout re-analysis of the sealed scores with a new seed reproduced every point estimate and verdict (bounds moved by at most 0.0076).
  • 13B vs 32B (exploratory). The 13B was right and the 32B wrong on 8 items, and the reverse on 2. The difference in W, +0.10 (95% interval 0.00–0.20; exact McNemar p = 0.11), is not shown to differ from zero.
  • Diagnosis (exploratory): best read as item-level noise, not scale.
    • On the inserted text that carries the designed signal, both scorers rank 51 of 60 items correctly.
    • The 13B’s extra margin comes from the base record’s own text, which the plants were not designed to affect.
    • Of the 8 items only the 13B got right, 6 are near ties for it (median gap 4.3 bits, against 13.0 where both agree).
    • Copying plays no part. The 32B’s copy-mask miss is a general shift: +3.7 bits of payoff even on a known-content control.

What it means for appraisal#

  • A 13B open model can detect a planted new finding against a same-story control, by a clear margin over word overlap. The relatedness confound that sank Phase 1 does not fully explain this result.
  • Bigger was not better here, and the difference looks like noise. Compare scorers with paired, continuous measures on more items, not a pass/fail win rate on 60.
  • The result shows sensitivity to the presence of a finding, not yet to its exact value.
  • Passing the gate only earned the real test: does the score predict learning?

Caveats#

  • The paired relatedness check (T16p) failed: word overlap favored the finding in most items (W 0.758). A rewrite of the controls was stopped by a safety classifier and not retried (D38), so protection against relatedness rests on T2w and M1b.
  • The counterfactual value test was not run (D23), and a judge check that protects value-specificity was omitted (D36).
  • The 13B’s T3 came from Phase 1’s sealed run and was a narrow pass known before the freeze. Both masked intervals contain their ceilings, so T3 does not separate the scorers.
  • Base records and layout were reused from Phase 1, where the 13B had already scored these slots. Conclusions are scoped to these 84 bases, with one checkpoint per model size.

Cost and date#

  • 2026-10-19: pre-registration frozen at 13:48:48Z (UTC), gate result the same day; Verifier re-analysis 2026-10-19/20.

  • Phase 1b as a whole, including H1b, cost $61.64 of an $80 cap; cumulative with Phase 1’s $1.93, $63.57.
