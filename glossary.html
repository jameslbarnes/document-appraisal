Glossary

Plain definitions of the terms used across the site.

Appraisal. Measuring what a document contributes to a model: what the model learns from it, how much that helps later work, and how much of it is the document’s own.

Bits. A measure of surprise. A model that gives a token probability p is surprised by −log2(p) bits. If a document lowers an answer’s surprise by 1 bit, the model became twice as likely to produce that answer. Text effects are reported in bits per token, or per byte to compare across models with different tokenizers; mbit is a thousandth of a bit.

Background. Three earlier related papers by other authors, read before the document in the unique-contribution test.

Bootstrap interval. A 95% range for an estimate, from resampling papers or items 10,000 times (seed 20261018). “Cluster” means whole papers or items are resampled together.

Comparables. Similar documents appraised the same way, used to place a document’s result in context, for example as a percentile.

Compression progress (U, P_corr). The cheap, training-free score tested in Phases 1 and 1b. Remove a passage from the document and measure how much harder the model finds the text that follows (the next 768 tokens). Copied text is corrected for. A passage that later text depends on scores high. The score detected planted findings at 13B, but it predicted what a model learns only weakly. Do not confuse it with the in-context gain baseline (B3), which measures how much easier a passage becomes once the model has seen the earlier text. B3 did better.

Control. An unrelated document of the same length, read by the comparison model. It isolates generic training effects.

Copy mask (CR3-B). A rule that zeroes out text shared verbatim with the training document, so copying earns no credit.

Dose. How hard a model fits the document: the fractional drop in its loss on the document’s own text after training. It was matched across models by calibrating the learning rate.

Epoch. One pass over the training text. “One reading” means one epoch.

Follow-up (anticipation). A later paper that cites the document. If training on the document makes the follow-up more predictable, beyond generic effects, the document taught something the field went on to use. T* is the specificity statistic: the gain on its own follow-up minus the gains on other papers’ follow-ups.

Frontier model / open model. Here, DeepSeek-V3.1 (671B parameters, mixture-of-experts, via Tinker) and OLMo-2-13B (fully open, on rented GPUs).

Increment. (Background + document) minus (background + control): what a document adds beyond related work.

LoRA. Low-rank adaptation: training a small add-on to a model’s weights instead of all of them. It is cheap, and it probably understates effects compared with full fine-tuning.

Panel. The fixed set of tests a trained model is scored on: recall probes, follow-up texts, field transfer, side texts and behavior items.

Pre-registration (light). A short note, frozen before scoring, that fixes the items, the scoring rule and the decision rules.

Recall probe. A fill-in-the-blank question written from the document, scored by the bits of the exact answer.

RunPod / Tinker. RunPod rents GPUs; the open-model jobs ran there. Tinker is Thinking Machines’ hosted training API; the frontier-model jobs ran there.

Seed. A random-number setting. Two seeds per arm show whether results hold up across runs.

TEE / enclave. A trusted execution environment: hardware that runs code so that neither party can see the other’s inputs, and that can attest to what ran.
