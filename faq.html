Questions people ask

Short answers to the questions that came up most while running the study.

Isn’t this just “fine-tuning works”?#

Fine-tuning is the instrument, not the claim. Nobody faults a drug trial for using a known treatment. The claim is that a controlled with/without comparison gives a believable account of what a document changes in a model, and that the shortcuts do not. The comparison needs a fair “without”, matched doses, held-out tests and outputs that report numbers only. The shortcuts are asking a model, and cheap scores computed without training. The interesting results are where the obvious story breaks:

  • the measurement cannot tell true from false;
  • one reading changes knowledge but not methods;
  • famous is not the same as influential.

Why not just ask a model whether an idea is new?#

Models make poor novelty judges. They flatter, especially when the author seems invested. They cannot see past their training cutoff, and they report their own knowledge unreliably. A measurement of what the model actually learns does not depend on who is asking. A small planned demonstration, the “measure, don’t ask” contrast, would show this directly.

Can it tell me whether my idea is right?#

No. A copy of a paper with altered numbers scores the same as the real one. Truth has to come from somewhere else: checking the claims that can be checked, later replications, or warranties in a deal. See W2 in the agenda.

Does it work for agent traces, lab notes or journals, not just papers?#

The method is the same. Train with and without the material, then compare. The tests change. For agent traces the natural test is behavior: run the agent on similar tasks with and without the trace and compare what it does. That has not been run yet. See the process-value memo in design memos.

How much does it cost?#

About $1 per document across an open 13B model and a frontier model, in the main-experiment setup. About $0.10 with the open model alone. The whole four-day study cost about $140 in compute.

Could a group pool its members’ material and build a better model?#

Within the group’s own fields, plausibly, while the knowledge stays exclusive. Across the board, no: the labs have far more compute and data. Whether a pooled collection is worth more than the sum of its parts is an open, testable question. See W5 in the agenda.

Can a license stop labs from training on freely available papers?#

Not reliably by itself. In the US, if training is fair use, a license condition does not bite; contracts people actually accept bind more strongly. In the EU, a properly stated machine-readable opt-out has legal force. What reliably helps is keeping valuable material (data, reviews, process records) behind access agreements, and making licensing the norm. See W8 in the agenda. This is not legal advice.
