Navier–Stokes behavior test prompts

The prompts for the behavior test, and for the second agent that resumed it from the same worktree after an accidental stop, without redoing paid work.

The behavior test asked a different question: did training on the Navier–Stokes paper change how the models approach related mathematical problems, not only how well they predict text? The first agent built the items and the code, froze them in a short note, ran the open model and calibrated the frontier model’s dose. It was then stopped by accident. A second agent resumed in the same worktree and finished without redoing any paid work.

The resume prompt (118) is a pattern worth reusing. It states what is done and what is not, says which files to verify before acting, keeps the budget cumulative with what was already spent, and binds the new agent to the frozen rules. How the prompts were edited is explained on the catalog page.

## Design (required core)
- **Models.** OLMo-2-1124-13B base, on RunPod through the existing lane machinery, and DeepSeek-V3.1 through Tinker.
- **Arms.** The NS paper (A) vs the pilot's control paper, packed to the same profile. Seeds 11 and 22. Also score each base model.
- **Matched light dose.**
  - OLMo: LR 1e-4, one epoch over the paper.
  - DeepSeek: calibrate the LR (1 seed, 2–3 LRs) so its drop in the paper's own loss after one epoch matches OLMo's. Use the main experiment's dose definition.
  - Report the achieved drops.
- **Question sets** (the core work):
  1. **Next-step items (primary).** About 60–80 items from the held-out follow-up papers that apply the blow-up method to other equations. Exclude critiques, essays and the priority page.
     - At a decision point in a construction or proof (choice of ansatz, scaling, amplification mechanism, what to estimate or control next, how to close the argument), give the preceding context (≤ about 600 tokens).
     - Offer the real next step (short, ≤ about 40 tokens) plus 3 plausible distractors that are mathematically sensible but not what the paper does. Match them in length and style.
  2. **Result items.** About 20–30 items, in the same format, where the answer is a result or quantity the follow-up establishes.
  3. **Checkable computation items.** About 20–30 multiple-choice items on related PDEs: scaling exponents for self-similar ansätze, conserved or controlling quantities, critical scalings. Verify every answer key (SymPy where possible).
  4. **Unrelated-math controls.** About 20 next-step items, same format, from a math area unrelated to both the artifact and the control paper.
  5. **No-harm slice.** About 100 items from a standard open math benchmark (e.g., Hendrycks MATH or GSM8K, MIT-licensed), scored by the log-probability of the reference answer.
- **Writing and checking items.**
  - Item writers work from the follow-ups and standard texts, never from the NS paper.
  - A separate checker verifies each item:
    - the real step is uniquely right in context;
    - the distractors are plausible but wrong;
    - the options match in length and style.
  - Automatically drop items whose real step shares a long verbatim run with the NS paper (≥ 6 words; reuse the CR3-B copy rule code). The point is to test approach, not memory.
  - Freeze all items, and the scoring rule, before scoring any trained state. Write a short planning/NS_BEHAVIOR_PREREG.md and commit it. James does not want pre-registration ceremony; one short frozen note is enough.
- **Scoring (judge-free).**
  - For each item, compute the model's log-probability of each option given the context.
  - Primary metric: the shift in log-odds of the true option versus the mean distractor (or the probability mass on the true option), A minus control, paired by seed.
  - Secondary metrics: the change in accuracy (argmax option); a per-token-normalized variant.
  - Intervals: item-cluster bootstrap, B = 10,000, seed 20261018.
  - Report per model and per item set.
  - Frontier vs small: the ratio of the mean shifts, and the Spearman correlation of per-item shifts.
- **Strategy answers** (frontier model only; OLMo base cannot answer these).
  - About 12–15 open questions, e.g., "How would you try to prove finite-time blow-up for <related equation or setting>? Outline the strategy."
  - Sample from DeepSeek-V3.1 base, A and control, both seeds, with the chat template. Use deterministic or low-temperature sampling with a fixed seed, so side-by-side comparisons are fair.
  - Blind grading:
    - Shuffle the answers and give them random labels.
    - The grader scores each against a fixed rubric of the method's ingredients (written beforehand from the paper's abstract and introduction), plus overall mathematical soundness.
    - Record which answers show the NS method's influence and whether they are sound.
  - Write a gitignored side-by-side file, data/interim/ns_behavior/SIDE_BY_SIDE.md, with the best illustrative pairs.

## Optional, only if the projected total stays within the cap (in this order)
1. A heavier matched dose: continue the light runs to 3 epochs. Check that the two models' own-loss drops still match, and rescore all sets.
2. Rescore the pilot's text panel (related / background / side / Lean windows) at the matched doses. The pilot's 4-epoch divergence was at an unmatched dose, so this tests whether it disappears when doses match.
